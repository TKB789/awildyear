Tips & key

A quick reference for your animals

Key
Full color needs
  • Most of the day's half-hours marked (90% or more)
  • At least 1 water drop
Water

Every body is different. The 8 drops are your daily target, and each drop can be whatever amount you need — a glass, a bottle, or a cup of tea.

No tracking hours set? Water becomes that day's goal: fill all 8 drops for full color.

Making time

Sprint, then rest

25 minutes of work, 5 minutes off. Short scheduled breaks help you get more done.

Longer break every 90 min

After about three sprints, step away for 15 minutes.

Little and often

Short daily sessions stick better than one long cram.

Test yourself

Write what you remember first, then check your notes.

Move on breaks

Stand, stretch, refill your water.

Name the next step

"Load the data" is easier to start than "do homework."

Cook once, eat three times

One prep session covers busy weeknights.

Clean in 10-minute pieces

One small chore a day keeps the weekend free.

Finish early

Aim to submit a couple of hours before a deadline.

Plan tonight

Five minutes before bed: look over tomorrow's plan.

The animals

Each half-hour you mark counts toward the day’s color. Each animal is a month, and each geometric facet is a day. A new animal appears every month, and finished months stay. Every animal comes with a card: what makes it interesting, where it lives and, for threatened species, its IUCN Red List status. Endangered animals come first, so you meet them early.
